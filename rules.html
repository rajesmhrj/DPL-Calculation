How to play Marriage

Marriage is Nepal's favourite rummy: three decks, 21 cards each, and a joker that only the players who've shown their hand get to see. Here's how a hand goes, and how we turn it into points.

The deal

Shuffle three standard decks together: 156 cards. Two to five people can play; bigger tables add a fourth deck.

Everyone gets 21 cards. One card goes face down in the middle: that's the joker, called the tiplu, and nobody looks at it yet. The rest of the deck is the draw pile, and its top card is turned up to start the discard pile.

Your turn

Take one card, either the top of the draw pile or the card just discarded, then throw one card away. You always end your turn holding 21.

Play goes round the table until somebody's whole hand is made of sets.

Building sets

Every set is at least three cards. Early in the hand, only pure sets count:

Pure sequence
Three or more cards in a row, all the same suit.
Tunnela
Three identical cards: same rank and same suit. Only possible because there are three decks.

Once you're seen (next section), joker cards can usually stand in for missing cards in your remaining sets. How freely depends on the table.

Getting seen

When you hold three pure sets, lay them down on your turn. You're now seen, and you get to look at the joker card in the middle.

Being seen matters twice over: only seen players can count maal, and an unseen player pays more when someone else finishes.

Tiplu and maal

The joker card is the tiplu. It and its neighbours are maal, and every point of maal you hold is money from each other player.

CardWhat it isMaal
TipluThe joker card itself3
JhipluOne below the tiplu, same suit2
PopluOne above the tiplu, same suit2
AlterSame rank and colour as the tiplu, other suit5
MarriageJhiplu, tiplu and poplu together in one sequence10

These are the common values. Plenty of tables add their own, such as maal for a tunnela you showed. Agree before the first deal.

Superman and Putalis

We shuffle four power cards in with the three decks: one Superman and three Putalis.

CardWhenMaal

Three Putalis in one hand: agree the value at your table before you start.

Playing dublee

Instead of sequences, you can collect dublees: pairs of identical cards. Show seven dublees and you're seen.

At our table a dublee player doesn't pay game points when someone else finishes, and if you finish on dublee everyone pays you 5 extra.

Finishing a hand

When all your cards are in sets, declare. You've won the hand, and everyone else is marked by how they ended it:

Seen
Showed three sets. Counts maal, pays the winner 3 game points.
Unseen
Never showed. Pays the winner 10 game points.
Dublee
Seen on dublees. Counts maal, pays no game points.
Foul
Declared a hand that wasn't valid. Loses their maal and pays 15.
Hold
Sat this hand out. Scores 0.

Murder and kidnap

What happens to an unseen player's maal is the big house rule:

Murder
Their maal counts for nothing. This is how we usually play.
Kidnap
The winner takes it and adds it to their own.

Scoring a hand

Everyone settles maal with everyone else. If n people played the hand:

points = n × your maal − total maal − game points you pay

The winner collects everyone's game points, so the hand always adds up to zero. Try it with a real hand from our table:

    From points to rupees

    Points become money at whatever the table agrees per point. We play at Rs. 10. A night of hands adds up to a final scoreboard, and that's what goes into the ledger, which keeps a running total until everyone settles.
